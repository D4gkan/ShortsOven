# SOCIAL STORY GENERATOR

Create one highly engaging social-media post built around a story, fact, anecdote, conversation, explanation, or discovery that makes someone stop scrolling and want to hear the rest.

The result must feel like a real post someone naturally encountered online, not an infographic, advertisement, news graphic, meme template, or AI-designed content card.

## 1. STORY FIRST

The story is the most important element.

Choose the subject freely and independently for every generation. The subject may come from history, science, technology, culture, everyday life, entertainment, human behavior, unusual events, personal experiences, the internet, or another naturally interesting area.

The story should produce one strong response: curiosity, amusement, fascination, disbelief, warmth, awkwardness, surprise, unease, or another emotion appropriate to the subject.

Do not force comedy, darkness, shock, or wholesomeness when the story does not naturally support it.

Do not rely on a fixed catalog of story ideas. Invent the subject and angle fresh every time.

## 2. ORIGINALITY

Generate the underlying subject BEFORE deciding how to present it.

Do not imitate, paraphrase, reskin, or make minor variations of stories from the prompt, previous generations, familiar viral posts, or common examples.

Avoid repeatedly returning to the same people, industries, historical periods, objects, situations, twists, or types of trivia.

A new generation should differ at the conceptual level, not merely by changing names, numbers, locations, or objects.

## 3. THE HOOK

The opening sentence should immediately create a reason to continue reading.

A strong hook usually introduces an unanswered question, unusual decision, contradiction, specific problem, surprising observation, strange event, or unexpected relationship between otherwise ordinary things.

Begin directly with the interesting material.

Avoid announcing that something is interesting, unbelievable, shocking, crazy, amazing, or surprising. Let the information create that reaction itself.

## 4. STORY DEVELOPMENT

Let the structure emerge naturally from the subject.

The story should generally move from an immediately interesting setup into context, increasingly meaningful or surprising information, and a satisfying payoff, consequence, explanation, revelation, or final detail.

Not every story needs a twist.

Not every story needs a conclusion.

Not every story needs the same pacing.

Do not withhold basic information artificially just to manufacture suspense.

Each new sentence should either advance the story, clarify something important, or make the situation more interesting.

Remove sentences that merely repeat or summarize what has already been established.

## 5. WRITING STYLE

Write like an intelligent person naturally telling other people something genuinely worth sharing.

Use conversational English, concrete details, varied sentence lengths, natural transitions, and appropriate quotations or dialogue when they genuinely improve the story.

Specificity is important. Names, quantities, durations, objects, locations, actions, and other details should be precise when precision makes sense.

Do not manufacture arbitrary specificity solely to make fictional information appear factual.

Avoid:

- corporate writing
- motivational language
- generic clickbait
- excessive adjectives
- repetitive dramatic fragments
- forced jokes
- explaining jokes
- fake profound conclusions
- generic AI phrasing
- unnecessary summaries
- repetitive rhetorical questions

The writing should sound authored rather than generated.

## 6. FACTUAL AND PERSONAL STORIES

When presenting something as factual, historical, scientific, biographical, or otherwise real, do not knowingly fabricate the central claim or supporting evidence.

Do not invent damaging allegations, crimes, scandals, quotations, or misconduct involving identifiable real people.

Invented personal anecdotes are allowed when appropriate, but they must behave like plausible human experiences rather than elaborate fiction disguised as documentary evidence.

## 7. SOCIAL-MEDIA PRESENTATION

Choose the social platform and post format that naturally fit the generated story.

Vary the platform and presentation between generations.

Reproduce the recognizable visual language of the selected platform convincingly, including typography, spacing, hierarchy, avatar placement, account information, line wrapping, margins, replies, and other necessary interface elements.

The result should look like an actual screenshot from a social-media feed.

Do not create a custom graphic that merely resembles social media.

Do not add unnecessary interface elements.

## 8. ACCOUNT IDENTIFIERS

Every username must contain at least one letter and either a digit or underscore.

If a separate display name appears, it must contain at least one letter and at least one digit, and must not contain an underscore.

Account identifiers should appear natural for the selected platform.

These identifier constraints are mandatory because usernames and display names are removed from narration by downstream processing.

## 9. VISUAL ARTIFACTS

An image or other visual artifact is optional.

Include one only when it materially improves the story, provides useful evidence, documents the subject, or makes the post substantially more interesting.

The artifact must directly relate to the actual subject of the story.

Never insert generic stock imagery simply to fill space.

Do not manufacture unrelated visual jokes or background events.

Photographs should match their supposed source and context. Casual photographs should retain believable imperfections rather than looking cinematic, staged, professionally lit, or artificially polished.

Historical or documentary material should visually fit its claimed context.

## 10. TEXT INSIDE PHOTOGRAPHS

Avoid readable generated text inside photographic artifacts unless written information is genuinely essential to the story.

The social-media interface itself may contain readable text.

This restriction reduces OCR contamination and visual-generation errors.

## 11. COMMENTS AND REPLIES

Comments and replies are optional.

Include them only when they add meaningful information, context, humor, clarification, contradiction, or another worthwhile layer to the story.

Never add comments merely because social-media posts normally have comments.

Avoid empty reactions that contribute nothing.

A post may contain no replies at all when the main story is already complete.

## 12. OCR AND NARRATION

The finished screenshot will be processed by OCR and converted into spoken narration.

Optimize the composition accordingly.

Story text must be clearly readable, sufficiently large, logically ordered, and visually separated from irrelevant interface elements.

Avoid decorative text, overlapping text, tiny writing, excessive interface labels, or anything likely to enter OCR without contributing to the narration.

Never show:

- engagement counts
- follower counts
- dates
- timestamps
- time-ago labels
- unrelated numeric interface metadata

Usernames and display names are permitted because downstream processing removes identifiers matching the required format.

Natural informal language and slang may be used when appropriate, but do not deliberately introduce spelling errors simply to imitate social media.

## 13. VISUAL FORMAT

The final output must use a strict 3:4 portrait aspect ratio.

The post should make effective use of the available frame without excessive empty margins.

Use a visually authentic light or dark interface appropriate to the selected platform.

Do not use decorative backgrounds, gradients, meme captions, arrows, circles, reaction stickers, giant headline graphics, watermarks, or artificial content-card styling unless such an element is genuinely native and necessary to the selected platform.

## 14. GENERATION DIVERSITY

Treat every generation as independent.

Before choosing a subject, mentally discard the obvious first ideas and search for a less predictable direction.

Vary the subject domain, emotional effect, narrative structure, platform, narrator perspective, artifact type, story length, pacing, and presentation.

Do not establish recurring formulas.

Do not simply substitute new nouns into the structure of a previous generation.

Do not let recently generated subjects disproportionately influence the next subject.

The collection should feel like posts discovered across the internet rather than posts written by one content generator.

## FINAL CHECK

Before producing the result, silently verify that:

- the opening creates immediate curiosity;
- there is enough substance to justify the post;
- the story progresses instead of repeating itself;
- details are specific where appropriate;
- the strongest information appears at an effective point;
- the writing sounds naturally human;
- the selected social platform suits the content;
- the interface looks convincingly authentic;
- any visual artifact directly supports the story;
- account identifiers satisfy the cleanup requirements;
- unnecessary OCR noise is absent;
- the output is strictly 3:4;
- the concept is substantially different from recent generations;
- the story was generated from abstract criteria rather than by copying or reskinning an example.
- Do NOT use em dashes

If any condition fails, revise the generation before producing the final result.
