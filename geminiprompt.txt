# Reddit/Viral Story Image Generation Prompt — v2

This is a two-part system:
1. **The image generation prompt** (formatting rules — mostly unchanged, they were already solid)
2. **The story engineering layer** (new — this is where the virality upgrades live)

---

## PART 1: FORMAT RULES (unchanged from your original — these were already tight)

Generate a new image from this text description. There is no source image or photo involved — this is a pure text-to-image creation task.

**FORMAT:** Flat 2D digital UI mockup, not a photograph. No phone, hand, table, glare, reflection, bezel, or camera bokeh. The image IS the raw screen content, edge-to-edge, like a cropped screenshot file.

**WORD COUNT:** Story text must be 60–80 words. Count it. If under 60, expand. If over 80, cut. This is non-negotiable.

**NO EM DASHES** anywhere in story or comment text. Use periods, commas, or rewrite.

**NO "Comments" LABEL** — separate story from comments with a single thin grey horizontal line.

**ONE IDENTIFIER PER COMMENTER** — single fictional username (underscore or number in it), no separate display name.

**VERTICAL PHONE FORMAT** — 9:16 to 3:4 range, nothing cropped, small natural top/bottom margin.

**NO "@" SYMBOL** before any username.

**PROFILE PICTURES FOR EVERYONE** — left of username, same vertical height, own distinct fictional avatar per person, no real faces.

**USERNAME ON ITS OWN LINE** — never shares a line with post/comment text.

**FRESH RANDOM USERNAMES EVERY TIME** — mix themes (nature, objects, numbers, moods, syllables), never reuse stock-sounding patterns.

**NEVER SHOW:** status bar, battery, wifi, clock, nav UI, gesture bar, timestamps, likes/shares/views, follower counts, verification badges, community notes.

**STYLE:** Realistic typography, authentic spacing, crisp text, natural line wrapping, random dark/light mode, high resolution.

## PART 2: STORY ENGINEERING LAYER (new)

**Standing rule:** Everything below describes structures and patterns, not example content. Never copy, lightly reword, or reuse specific phrases, scenarios, character details, or wording found anywhere in this document. Every generated story must be entirely original.

### Step 1 — Pick a niche (weighted rotation)

Roll for niche before writing. Don't let the same niche repeat back to back.

| Weight | Niche | What it delivers |
|---|---|---|
| 22% | **Wholesome / Heartwarming** | Emotional resonance, tear-jerker payoff, cross-generational shareability |
| 20% | **Relatable Everyday Humor** | "This is SO me" tagging, low-stakes universal embarrassment |
| 18% | **Dark / Absurdist Comedy** | Shock + laugh, subverted expectations, cynical punchline |
| 12% | **"What If" Hypothetical** | Interactive — comments explode with people answering |
| 10% | **Sci-Fi / Speculative** | Nerd-bait, invites theorycrafting and debate |
| 8% | **Observational Social Commentary** | Taps a real frustration, people share to vent collectively |
| 6% | **Nostalgia / Childhood Memory** | Shared cultural memory, "back in my day" energy |
| 4% | **Meta / Creative Process** | Insider-y, appeals to writers/teachers/editors specifically |

### Step 2 — Choose a hook formula (first sentence)

Pick ONE structural pattern, don't blend multiple. Invent fresh, unique wording every time. Do not reuse stock phrasing, sentence templates, or specific scenarios across generations. These are structural patterns to follow, not lines to copy or lightly reword.

- **The Bait-and-Switch Confession:** Someone warns the narrator not to be offended by what they're about to say or do, then what follows turns out to be the opposite of offensive. The mismatch between the warning and the actual content is the hook.
- **The Boring Setup:** Open with a mundane, specific, low-stakes situation (an errand, a chore, a routine moment). The payoff lands harder precisely because the setup asked nothing of the reader.
- **The Today-Years-Old Realization:** Open on the narrator just now learning or realizing something that reframes a piece of their past.
- **The Overheard Punchline:** Open mid-scene on a quoted line of dialogue that makes no sense out of context yet, forcing the reader to keep going to understand it.
- **The Hypothetical Direct-Address:** Speak straight to the reader with a scenario or rule ("you" framing), forcing mental simulation of what they'd do.
- **The Time-Stamped Memory:** Open by signaling this is a specific remembered incident being retold, then immediately name the absurd or unusual event at the center of it.
- **The Small Detail That Implies Everything:** Open on one tiny, concrete, ordinary-sounding detail that quietly signals larger emotional stakes without stating them directly. The detail should never be explained.

Vary sentence structure, subject matter, setting, and specific details completely from generation to generation. Two stories using the same formula should not resemble each other beyond sharing the underlying structural logic.

### Step 3 — Build in a "projection line" (mandatory)

Every story needs exactly ONE sentence engineered to make a reader think **"that's me"** or **"I wish that were me."** This is the single highest-leverage sentence in the story — it's what turns a passive read into a comment.

Rules for the projection line:
- It should be a small, specific, human detail — not a moral or a lesson.
- It works best when it's slightly vulnerable, slightly dark, or slightly unresolved (a missing parent, a moment of loneliness, an act of unexpected kindness, a small cruelty that was actually deserved).
- Don't explain it or lean on it. State it plainly and move on — let the reader do the emotional work. Under-explaining is what makes people fill the gap with their own life in the comments.
- It does NOT need to be the twist. It can sit earlier in the story as a quiet detail the twist later recontextualizes.

The strongest projection lines tend to touch on: absence (a parent, a friend, a person who should be there but isn't), an unexpected act of kindness or cruelty, a moment of being unseen or overlooked, or a small mercy in an otherwise hard situation. Pick a fresh scenario each time. Never reuse the same relationship, situation, or wording across generations.

### Step 4 — Structure

Hook → curiosity → escalation → escalation → complication → false expectation → **projection line** → twist → payoff.

- First sentence hooks instantly.
- Every sentence adds curiosity, raises stakes, reveals info, or moves toward the ending. No filler, no repetition, no over-explaining.
- Ending is surprising yet inevitable in hindsight — never a moral, never "and that's why..."
- Sounds like a real person recounting something that actually happened, not a written short story.

### Step 5 — Comments (engineered, not random)

Randomly choose: no comments (25%), one comment (30%), two comments (30%), three comments (15%) — max 3.

When comments are included, don't pick jokes at random. Fill these roles, in this priority order:

1. **The Vulnerable Reply** (highest priority if only 1 comment) — a comment that responds emotionally or personally to the projection line, as if the commenter recognized something from their own life in it. This is the comment most likely to spark its own reply-chain of people relating.
2. **The Callback Joke** — references a specific detail from the story, rewards people who read closely.
3. **The Dissent / Alternate Take** — mildly disagrees, offers a different read, or one-ups the story. Creates a small argument in the replies without being genuinely toxic.

Comments do not count toward the 60–80 word story limit. Same em-dash, username, and avatar rules apply.

### Step 6 — Pre-generation virality gut-check

Before generating the image, verify:

- [ ] Does the story have a **wound** (a small vulnerable/relatable detail, the projection line)?
- [ ] Does it have a **twist or unresolved tension** at the end, not a moral?
- [ ] Is there a clear reason someone would **comment** rather than just scroll past (something to relate to, defend, answer, or react to)?
- [ ] Is the hook one of the proven formulas above, and does it land in the first sentence?
- [ ] Word count 60–80, verified by counting?
- [ ] No em dashes, no "@", no "Comments" label?
- [ ] Comments (if any) fill the Vulnerable/Callback/Dissent roles rather than being random jokes?

If any box fails, revise before generating.

## PART 3: THINGS TO AVOID (from your own data)

| Killer | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Long setup, no payoff | Shorts attention span is ~3 seconds to hook |
| No emotional trigger / flat storytelling | Gets swiped past |
| Over-complex premise | Audience won't do the work to follow it |
| No relatability or "what if" angle | Nothing for the viewer to personally attach to |
| Generic advice / motivational-quote energy | Seen a million times, instantly boring |
| Explaining the emotional beat instead of just stating it | Kills the projection effect — let the reader feel it themselves |
